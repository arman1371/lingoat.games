# Grammar lesson sources

The full grammar lessons (`src/content/grammar/`) are written in our own words. Every rule was checked against the
pages below, which were fetched and read while writing; the same list is kept with each topic (`sources`, not shown
on the lesson page). The German content has **not yet been reviewed by a native speaker**.

Forms in tables are generated from `src/lib/german` where possible (articles, conjugation, Präteritum, Konjunktiv II,
ordinals), so they are covered by the unit tests of those helpers.

Limits for C1: the Wikipedia pages confirm the structures but several of them do not cover the details. These points follow common
textbook rules (the C1 course books in `docs/c1-1-coverage.md` and `docs/c1-2-coverage.md`) and were **not** confirmed by a fetched page:

- the subjective meanings of the modal verbs (the Modalverb page does not cover them) and the infinitive perfect with them;
- the uninflected position of intensifiers and the ranking of ziemlich / äußerst / höchst (Gradpartikel names only some of them);
- `es sei denn` and `außer wenn`, `nur dass`, `so sehr … auch` (not on the Konzessivsatz page), verb-first conditions (Sollte …, Hätte …);
- adjectives with fixed prepositions (the Rektion page has no examples), the fraction word `Hälfte` and `um` / `auf` with changes;
- the passive substitutes `sich lassen`, `bekommen`-Passiv and `-bar` (the Passiv and Passiversatz pages could not be fetched);
- the Duden and Deutsche Welle pages were not used.

C1 units that use topics first taught at an earlier level (Konjunktiv I and noun-verb phrases in `c1-1-sprache` / `c1-1-manieren`,
word-formation, `lassen`, genitive prepositions, the passive) link those topics once the lessons of the earlier levels are on main.

## Limits of the research

- The Deutsche Welle learner pages could not be fetched, and the Duden grammar pages are not freely readable, so the
  lessons lean on Wikipedia and Wiktionary for rules. Details those pages do not state (for example the order of
  dative and accusative pronouns, or the vowel change of the modal verbs) follow common textbook rules and the
  generated forms in `src/lib/german`.
- A native speaker (or a second authoritative grammar such as the Duden Grammatik) should still review each topic
  before the lessons are treated as final.
