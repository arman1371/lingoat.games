# Grammar lesson sources

The full grammar lessons (`src/content/grammar/`) are written in our own words. Every rule was checked against the
pages below, which were fetched and read while writing; the same list is kept with each topic (`sources`, not shown
on the lesson page). The German content has **not yet been reviewed by a native speaker**.

Forms in tables are generated from `src/lib/german` where possible (articles, conjugation, Präteritum, Konjunktiv II,
ordinals), so they are covered by the unit tests of those helpers.

## Limits of the research

- The Deutsche Welle learner pages could not be fetched, and the Duden grammar pages are not freely readable, so the
  lessons lean on Wikipedia and Wiktionary for rules. Details those pages do not state (for example the order of
  dative and accusative pronouns, or the vowel change of the modal verbs) follow common textbook rules and the
  generated forms in `src/lib/german`.
- A native speaker (or a second authoritative grammar such as the Duden Grammatik) should still review each topic
  before the lessons are treated as final.
- B1: the Wikipedia and Wiktionary pages do not state every rule the lessons give. These follow common textbook rules
  and the generated forms in `src/lib/german`: haben / sein with the Plusquamperfekt, the Perfekt in the main clause
  after nachdem in speech, same or different subject with um / ohne / statt … zu versus damit / ohne dass / statt dass,
  `als` + verb without `ob`, `durch` + accusative for the cause in the passive, `wo(r)` + preposition after `alles`,
  the position of `nicht` before prepositional phrases and adverbs, and the nicht / kein choice.
- **B2 limits:** the Wikipedia pages on the Mittelfeld, the epistemic use of modal verbs and the Duden grammar could not
  be fetched or did not cover the point. These rules therefore follow common textbook teaching and are **not** checked
  against a fetched page: the te-ka-mo-lo order (a university handout and a learner site were read instead), adjective
  endings after manche and sämtliche, the aus / vor distinction, von / durch for the agent in the Zustandspassiv,
  the factual vs. hypothetical split of obwohl / auch wenn / selbst wenn, the Plusquamperfekt after als in comparisons,
  and the plural -keit / -heit / -ung gender rule. Wiktionary pages state the case and meaning of each connector, not
  its word order, so the verb position of connectors comes from the example sentences and from general German grammar.
